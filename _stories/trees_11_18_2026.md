I've always been fascinated by trees. 

It was two summers ago, I was on a road trip with my best friend. I was sitting on a rock, looking, listening. All I could hear was the wind in the branches. At that moment, I was struck with the realization of how wonderful trees are, and how _wise_. They stood there unmoving, interacting with the natural world around it in a way that felt admirable. Not overstepping, not overcompensating, but doing their due diligence. I thought about the fact that to enjoy their presence is the exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide, a subconscious processes that we don't even think about, and in it the trees engage with us.

I was also struck by their independence and at the same time, their community. They do not rush, they do not have a human timeline. We don’t judge them for how slow they grow. Each season can bring leaves falling, leaving branches bare. There are seasons of blooming and seasons of rest, seasons recovering and seasons of bearing fruit. But it’s not a flaw, it is how they were made to function. Since then trees have been a constant reminder that we do not have to rush, we do not have to be anything other than what we are. 

I have also included one of the many poems I have written about trees since then: 

the curve of fingerprints
with trees we seem to share
the intertwining roots
I watch you brush your hair
we are not so different, you and I
you give me shade 
you give me air.
